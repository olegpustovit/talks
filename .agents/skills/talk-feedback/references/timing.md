# When to give which feedback

Feedback that is right a week out is harmful the night before. Pick a
mode and stay in it.

## Week before (default for an ungiven talk)

**Goal:** make the talk true.

Allowed: rewrite the thesis, reorder acts, cut whole slides, replace a
dead demo with a live one, throw out Photomaniac images, add expansion
joints, demand a proof artifact.

Forbidden: polishing animations, font nits, "smile more".

Rehearsal at this distance should be in front of someone who has not
seen the talk. "Rehearsing in your head" does not count (conference
organizers who force a two-week dry run are right).

## Day before

**Goal:** help the speaker deliver the talk they have.

Allowed: pacing, which beat to pause on, a line that is hard to say,
font size that will fail a back row, a backup if the live demo dies,
speaker-note stage directions.

Forbidden: rearranging the deck, adding a new act, "have you considered
a different thesis". That ship has sailed. Lara Hogan's advice applies:
do not grind the talk until it feels stale; a day-before rewrite
creates a talk the speaker has not rehearsed.

## Immediately after the live talk

**Goal:** the speaker can still hear themselves as competent.

Allowed: **only specific good**. Diamonds. "When you paused on the two
writes, the room leaned in." No sandwich, no "but", no notes for next
time. They cannot use structural notes tonight and they will only
remember the sting.

This is TED-Ed's "gift" mode with the constructive slice removed on
purpose. Save the spades for the recording.

## After a recording (or the next morning)

**Goal:** a full debrief the speaker can apply to the next delivery or
the next talk.

Allowed: the entire rubric. Watch the recording once for the argument,
once for the slides, once for the demo. Timestamp findings. Keep the
Monday list to three items even if you noticed thirty.

Compare what was *said* to what `RESEARCH.md` claimed. Drift is a
finding.

## How to say it

Adapted from TED-Ed Clubs / LifeLabs "playing cards" and ASCO SMART
feedback:

| Card | Example | Use? |
|------|---------|------|
| Heart (positive, vague) | "Loved the intro" | No |
| Diamond (positive, specific) | "The cron slide is the obvious solution; the next slide is the crash. That pair is the talk." | Yes |
| Club (negative, vague) | "The Temporal part was a lot" | No |
| Spade (negative, specific + a way out) | "Slides 13–20 are a product tour. Keep 13, 14, 19; move 16 and 18 into the cut list in RESEARCH.md." | Yes |

Other rules that actually change behavior:

- **Specific, timely, evidence-based.** Cite the slide title or a
  timestamp, not "the middle".
- **Describe, don't judge.** "The title is 'Overview'" not "this slide
  is lazy".
- **No 'but'.** A diamond and a spade are two sentences, not one
  cancelled compliment.
- **Own the view.** "I could not reconstruct the thesis from the titles
  alone" not "the thesis is missing".
- **Achievable.** A Monday list of three file-level edits, not
  "be more inspiring".

## What this skill is not

Not voice coaching, not "um" counts, not clothing, not an audience
survey. Delivery notes are limited to what the artifact can support
(notes that fight the slide, a demo with no fallback, a 40-minute
script in a 25-minute slot).
